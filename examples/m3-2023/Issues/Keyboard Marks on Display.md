# Keyboard Marks on Display

Faint keyboard-key outlines, smudges, or rub marks transfer onto the screen when the lid is closed under pressure. Cosmetic and confirmed across multiple outlets, but it is a general thin-lid MacBook trait, not an M3-specific batch defect.

## Symptoms

- Faint keyboard-key outlines, smudges, or rub marks on the screen, sometimes anti-reflective-coating wear, after the lid is closed under pressure (for example, in a tight bag).
- Most marks wipe off. Only a minority progress to permanent AR-coating abrasion or pitting.

## Suspected cause

- Very tight lid-to-keyboard tolerance (reported around two sheets of paper) lets keys contact the panel under pressure; oils and grit transfer, and over time can abrade the AR coating.
- Largely usage- and pressure-driven rather than a batch defect. Best framed as an inherent thin-lid MacBook design behavior carried into M3: Apple and the community treat light marking as normal, and there is a whole product category of screen-imprint liners (UPPERCASE GhostBlanket, RadTech) built around it.

## Affected scope

- MacBook Pro 14"/16" M3 / M3 Pro / M3 Max, and carried from prior MacBook Pro generations (corroborated on M1 and 2021 14"/16" MBP, and on MacBook Air).
- Some units affected, not all. Confirmed by multiple outlets, so this is documented as a known design trait to set buyer expectations, not as an M3 QA failure.

## How to detect on-site

Maps to **Phase 6** (display visual inspection) and **Phase 7** (manual physical inspection). Open the lid and view the screen at an angle under bright light against a white background, looking for key-pattern marks or AR-coating haze. On a used unit this is a wear indicator. Clean gently first and inspect for permanent AR-coating damage versus removable smudge.
