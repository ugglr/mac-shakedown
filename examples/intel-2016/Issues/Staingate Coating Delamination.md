# Staingate Anti-Reflective Coating Delamination

Stain-like blotches where the Retina anti-reflective coating wears off. Predominantly a 2012-2015 phenomenon, so mostly out-of-scope for 2016+ units, carried as historical context.

## Symptoms

- Stain-like blotches where the anti-reflective coating wears off or delaminates.
- Most visible at low brightness, with the screen off, or in sunlight.
- Often starts near keyboard / trackpad contact points.

## Suspected cause

Anti-reflective coating adhesion failure on Retina panels, accelerated by contact, cleaning, and oils. This was an industry-wide coating issue, not specific to one chassis. Apple ran a (quietly handled, never publicly posted) anti-reflective coating repair program as an implicit acknowledgment.

## Affected scope

Retina MacBook and MacBook Pro displays, predominantly 2012-2015 models, plus the 12" MacBook 2015-2017. Mostly PRE-scope, with very few 2016+ reports. Apple's coating program covered units back to 2012 but dropped 2013-2014 models in December 2019 and is effectively closed. Essentially out-of-scope for modern 2016+ butterfly/T2 machines, of historical relevance only. Severity is low: cosmetic, not data-loss or safety.

## How to detect on-site

This maps to **manual Phase 6 (display inspection under angled light)** and is not automatable. Tilt the display under bright, angled light and view a white or off screen for blotchy worn patches in the coating. Mostly relevant only if cross-shopping 2012-2015 stock, and low priority for in-scope 2016+ units.
