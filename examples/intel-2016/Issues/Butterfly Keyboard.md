# Butterfly Keyboard Sticky / Repeating / Dead Keys

**The single most important physical check on any pre-2019 unit.** A true reliability disaster spanning 2015/2016-2019, with its own multi-year Apple Keyboard Service Program (now ended).

## Symptoms

- Individual keys that do not register a press.
- Keys that repeat or double a character from a single press.
- Sticky keys, or keys with inconsistent feel.
- Most user-noticeable on space, E, O, and other high-use keys.

## Suspected cause

The ultra-thin butterfly switch mechanism is intolerant of debris ingress. Apple itself stated the mechanism "can get jammed up with crumbs, dust and other particulates." A single dust particle under a key can disable or double it. The mechanism is also less serviceable: repair meant a full top-case replacement. The 2018+ Gen-3 membrane reduced but did not eliminate the problem (Apple apologized for 2018 keyboard failures), so a 2018-2019 unit is improved, not immune.

## Affected scope

MacBook 12" (2015-2017), MacBook Pro 13"/15" 2016-2019 (including Touch Bar models), MacBook Air 2018-2019. Gen 1-2 (2016-2017) are worst. All were covered by the Keyboard Service Program, which officially ended in November 2024, so repairs are now out-of-pocket. A $50M US class-action settlement (final approval May 2023) confirms the defect was widespread and legally established, not anecdotal.

## How to detect on-site

This maps to **manual Phase 7 (keyboard step 2)**, and is not detectable by any automated phase. Phase 7 keyboard testing should be extra-thorough on butterfly-era units. Open Notes or any text field and type the full alphabet, numbers, and space repeatedly, plus modifier combos several times. Watch for any non-registering, doubling, or sticky key. Inspect for backlight uniformity while you are there.
